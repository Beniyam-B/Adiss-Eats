The Screens and What Each Needs

Home is the landing page, showing today's chef-picked specials and a featured "centerpiece" dish; it needs the specials list and the full menu (for a drinks upsell row and Recently Viewed) from the live API. Full Menu is where a user browses everything, searching, filtering by category or fasting status, and sorting by price or name; it needs the complete menu list. Dish Detail shows one dish fully — description, spice level, ingredients, and customization options — and needs that one dish's full record plus a short list of other dishes to suggest as pairings. Login and Register let a user sign in or create an account, needing only a name and phone number (register additionally takes an email and password) — no data from outside the app itself. Cart shows everything currently added, needing the user's current cart contents and any applied coupon to calculate a live total. Checkout (reachable only when signed in) needs the same cart contents, plus whichever fulfillment method (pickup or delivery) the user selects, to calculate the final total and confirm the order. Legal is a static page needing no data at all. A catch-all 404 page handles any URL that doesn't match, with a link back to the home page.



Route map: The app’s route flow is simple. Home is at / and renders the Home page from src/pages/Home.jsx. The Full Menu is at /menu and loads FullMenu from src/pages/FullMenu.jsx. Dish detail is a dynamic /dish/:dishId route that renders DishHighlight from src/pages/DishHighlight.jsx. Login and Register are at /login and /register, and they live in src/pages/Login.jsx and src/pages/Register.jsx. Cart is at /cart and shows the cart page, which wraps CartView from src/componenets/CartView.jsx. Checkout is at /checkout and renders Checkout from src/pages/Checkout.jsx, but it is protected and only available to signed-in users. Legal is at /legal, and any URL that does not match a route falls through to NotFound in src/pages/NotFound.jsx with a link back to home. All routes live in one persistent layout with the header, footer, and toast.

Page by page: Home is the landing page with the sticky header, hero, clickable centerpiece dish, live specials with category filtering, a story band, drinks upsell, Recently Viewed section, testimonials, and closing CTA. Full Menu is the live data browser with search, fasting filter, category chips, and sorting. Dish Highlight is the product detail page for /dish/:id, with full description, peppers for real heat ratings, live-priced customization, and paired-item suggestions. Login and Register are the form-based auth screens with react-hook-form and Zod validation; login checks against the registered users stored in the auth store, which is why unregistered users can no longer sign in. Cart is the two-column list and sticky receipt, and each item’s Add to Cart button becomes a live stepper once it is in the cart. Checkout is protected, includes pickup vs. delivery with live address and fee behavior, and shares the same pricing logic as Cart. The dish detail modal closes with the X button, Escape, or the backdrop; the panel itself stops the click so the dialog does not close when you click inside it. Legal is the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service page.

Day 26–27: the project is built around props, children, conditional rendering, and map-driven lists. Props pass data like item and onViewDetails into MenuItemCard, children are used in Modal, and conditional rendering handles badges, loading states, and the login/logout swap. Keys are always real values such as item.id or cartLineId rather than array indexes, because the cart can reorder and re-render in ways that index keys would misattribute.

Day 28: state is local where it belongs. Search, sort, and filter live in FullMenu, the quantity stepper lives in DishHighlight, and the mobile nav toggle lives in Header. The clearest lifting-state example is selectedItem, which lives in the page component rather than inside each individual card because several sibling cards need to know which modal is open at the same time.

Day 29: useEffect and useRef are used for fetches and modal behavior. The shared loading/error/data logic lives in src/hooks/useFetch.js and is reused anywhere the app hits the live API. useRef is used in Modal.jsx to focus the dialog and remember the previously focused element, and the cleanup logic handles the modal keydown listener and focus restoration. The honest gap here is that useFetch does not use AbortController, but it is not necessary in this app because the same fetch is not rapidly retriggered in a way that would create a race condition.

Day 30: this is where context, custom hooks, and store-level thinking begin to split apart. RecentlyViewedContext is a simple useContext pattern with a small number of consumers, and useFetch is the app’s reusable custom hook. There is no useReducer in this rebuild because the cart became complex enough to live in a store, which matches the course guidance of preferring useState, then context, then a store. The same idea still shows up in the Zustand actions, just implemented as store actions rather than a reducer. There is also no useMemo or useCallback yet because the app has not been profiled as slow, and the guidance here is to memoize only when there is a demonstrated bottleneck.

Day 32: this is the Zustand moment. The cart and auth data live in src/store/useCartStore.js and src/store/useAuthStore.js, both created with zustand and persisted. They are read with narrow selectors so components only re-render when the exact value they use changes. That is the textbook case for a store instead of context, especially for cart and auth state that is shared across the header, cards, checkout, and modal. The auth store also validates against a registered users list, which is why login only works for already registered accounts.

Day 33: forms are built with react-hook-form, Zod, and @hookform/resolvers. The validation rules live in src/schemas/formSchemas.js, and the shared accessible field component in src/componenets/Field.jsx is reused across Login, Register, and Checkout. That pattern makes the form behavior consistent and keeps the validation and accessibility rules in one place.

Day 34: ErrorBoundary wraps the app, route-level code splitting is used for everything except Home, and Modal uses a portal into the modal root with a proper focus trap. The modal moves focus inside, traps Tab inside the dialog, closes on Escape, restores focus to the opener, and uses role="dialog" and aria-labelledby for accessibility. This is the same pattern the day calls for, even though the app keeps a single top-level boundary instead of splitting error boundaries by region.

The shared architecture is kept pretty simple. Zustand stores in src/store/useCartStore.js and src/store/useAuthStore.js handle the cart and auth without needing a Provider. The one remaining context is RecentlyViewedContext in src/context/RecentlyViewedContext.jsx. The form validation rules are in src/schemas/formSchemas.js, the shared accessible input is in src/componenets/Field.jsx, and the shared fetch pattern is in src/hooks/useFetch.js. Pricing and spice logic live in src/utils/pricing.js and src/utils/spice.js, DishImage handles its fallback in src/componenets/DishImage.jsx, and Modal handles the portal and focus trap in src/componenets/Modal.jsx.
